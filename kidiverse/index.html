The spine

One loop, every world

Every Kidiverse world runs the same four steps. A new world is mostly content, not mostly engineering — which is the entire reason the scene framework exists.

  1. 01

    Camera

    A phone propped across the room, landscape, watching the whole body. Frames are processed in memory on the phone and discarded.

  2. 02

    Body tracking

    Pose and hand landmarks — shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles. Twenty-two points, on-device. No face geometry, ever.

  3. 03

    Gesture

    Movement becomes meaning: gather, sweep, jump, spin, clear. Each one declares its own confirmation window, its refractory period, and who wins when two fire at once.

  4. 04

    The world answers

    Cloud thickens, wind turns, lightning forks, daylight returns. Fast enough that a child reads it as cause and effect — the whole difference between this and a movement video.

The library

Three worlds, one engine

Worlds two and three were built on the framework the first world earned. Same gesture recognition, same framing, same session lifecycle — different meaning.

World one

Weather Control

A valley, a sky, and a child who owns it. Arms overhead gather cloud until it is heavy enough to rain. A sweep takes the rain sideways. A jump cracks thunder. Arms lowered and spread bring the sun back.

The child sees themselves inside the scene, cut out from the room behind them.

World three

Weather Control 2

The child stands inside a sky and drives it. Spin a hand and a funnel winds up beside it. Sweep and the wind takes the rain sideways. Jump inside a storm you built yourself and it forks lightning.

The tornado has a home in the scene whether or not it can find your hand — which is why a television with no body data can still draw it correctly.

World two

Human Anatomy

A child stands in front of the camera and an anatomical figure moves with them. They press Skeleton, Muscles, Organs or Full Body, and the same tracked movement is shown through a different layer of the body.

Reach, crouch and turn cross into the world as meaning. Joint coordinates never do.

Growing with the child

The second surface

The phone watches.
The television plays.

Putting the world on the television is not a cosmetic upgrade. The child looks at a big screen at a natural playing distance instead of squinting at a propped phone, the phone is freed to be a dedicated sensor rather than sensor and display at once, and a shared screen is the only sensible surface for two children playing together.

It also puts the parent’s controls on the device the parent is already holding.

Camera frames and raw body data never leave the phone. Only the world’s own state crosses your network — and it never reaches the internet.

For parents

What the camera does,
in plain words

A camera pointed at a child is the biggest thing a parent will hesitate over, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a paragraph of policy. These are the words the app itself shows, before the phone asks for anything.

Your child plays by moving, and they see themselves in the game.

Kidiverse uses the camera to follow your child’s body — arms, legs, how they move — and to cut their picture out of the room so they appear inside the game world. That picture is the point of the game: they raise their arms and see themselves raise the storm.

Anyone the camera can see appears on screen, not just the child playing.

The camera cannot tell one person from another. It separates people from the room behind them, so anybody who walks into view appears in the game too. The only way not to appear is to stay out of the camera’s view.

Nothing is recorded. Nothing is saved. Nothing is sent anywhere.

The picture is shown on the screen and then it is gone. There is no recording, no photo, no video file, and nothing is uploaded to us or to anyone else. Closing the app leaves nothing behind.

You can say no, and you can stop at any time.

If you decline the camera, Kidiverse cannot run — the whole game is your child moving. Nothing else changes, and you can grant it later from your device settings.

  • Processed on the phone. There is no cloud vision pipeline, now or later.
  • Pose and hand landmarks only. No face geometry, no face mesh, no templates, and no facial recognition of any kind. Settled, not deferred.
  • No accounts, no ads, no third-party analytics. Built for the terms Apple’s Kids Category sets, which hold for every future update once you opt in.
  • Head points are discarded before they reach the game. Not filtered later — they never cross out of the tracker.
  • The shipping build cannot contain the tools we test with. Observer surfaces and the network transport are resolved out of it, so “no code path capable of transmitting camera imagery exists” is something an inspector can verify by reading the build, not a promise about intent.

Honest ledger

Where it is today

Kidiverse is in development. Rather than describe it as finished, here is the same split the team keeps internally — what has landed, and what has not been proven yet.

Built and tested

  • Three worlds on one shared scene framework.
  • Gesture recognition with declared confirmation windows, refractory periods and deterministic arbitration when two gestures fire at once.
  • Framing and onboarding — the “step back until you fit” moment treated as a designed feature rather than setup friction.
  • The parent-facing camera explanation, with a test that scans the whole repository for wording that has been ruled misleading.
  • A public build that provably cannot contain the validation tooling.
  • On-device tracking and rendering on iPhone.

Not proven yet

  • It has never run on the target Android device. Android is a gate, not a port, and that gate has not been passed.
  • No frame rate or latency figure has been measured on a named device — which is exactly why no such number appears anywhere on this page.
  • Device acceptance tests for the two newest worlds have not been performed.
  • The television cannot show a child until one open architectural decision is answered.
  • No store listing, no pricing, and no date.
